Marriage Tax Bonus / Penalty — 2026

Federal tax (income tax + Social Security + Medicare + Additional Medicare + NIIT) for two people filing Single versus Married Filing Jointly. Blue = marriage saves money, red = marriage costs money.

Not tax advice; accuracy is not guaranteed. See the disclaimer.

Display

Assumptions
  • Standard deduction only ($16,100 single, $32,200 joint). No credits, no state tax.
  • Investment income (short-term gains, dividends, interest) is taxed as ordinary income, is not subject to FICA, and counts toward NIIT.
  • All other income is wages. 401(k) lowers income tax but not FICA. HSA is assumed to go through payroll, so it lowers both. Contributions are capped at wages.
  • Each person's FICA is computed on their own wages. Social Security wage base is $184,500.
  • Additional Medicare 0.9% over $200k (single) / $250k (joint). NIIT 3.8% over $200k / $250k MAGI.
  • Marginal rate is the income-tax bracket on the next dollar, plus 3.8% when NIIT applies. It leaves out FICA. Effective rate is total tax (FICA included) divided by gross income.
  • 2026 limits: 401(k) $24,500 (+$8,000 age 50+, or +$11,250 age 60–63). HSA uses the self-only limit per person, $4,400 (+$1,000 age 55+); two self-only limits (~$8,800) approximate the $8,750 family limit.
