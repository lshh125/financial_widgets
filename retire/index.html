When to Start Social Security

The accumulated value of Social Security checks from age 62, for each starting age from 62 to 70, for someone with maxed-out earnings. Each check keeps growing at a "value rate" you choose, whether it is saved or spent, since money spent now saves buying the same things later at higher prices. Taxes and other benefits are not included.

Starting ages

Assumptions
  • Full retirement age 67 (born in 1960 or later). Starting earlier cuts the benefit by 5/9 of 1% a month for the first 36 months and 5/12 of 1% a month beyond that (70% at 62). Waiting past 67 adds 2/3 of 1% a month, up to 124% at 70.
  • The benefit at full retirement age is in today's dollars at age 62. COLAs raise it every year from 62 on, including years before you start.
  • Each month's check arrives at the end of the month. Value grows monthly at the yearly value rate's monthly equivalent.
  • Not included: income tax on benefits, the earnings test before full retirement age, spousal and survivor benefits, and Medicare premiums.
