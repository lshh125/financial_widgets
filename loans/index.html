Pay Off Loans or Invest? — 2026

Compares using cash to pay down loans early with investing it in a taxable brokerage account while keeping scheduled payments. Both strategies get the same monthly budget; anything a strategy does not need for loan payments is invested. Taxes are computed for each strategy under 2026 U.S. federal law.

What is modeled
  • Cash flow: each month both strategies have the same budget, equal to the scheduled loan payments and mortgage insurance with no prepayment, plus extra savings. A payment that ends (by payoff or prepayment) stays in the budget and is invested, or snowballed if you choose that.
  • Taxes: each year, full 2026 federal income tax + NIIT is computed for each strategy and compared with the same year without any loans or investments. This handles standard vs. itemized deductions, phaseouts, the SALT cap, the $750k mortgage limit, and the 2/37 limit for the 37% bracket. Tax changes are paid from, or added to, the brokerage account at year end.
  • Later years: 2026 brackets and limits are held fixed (no inflation adjustment). Changes already in law are applied: the car loan interest deduction ends after 2028 and the SALT cap returns to $10,000 in 2030. Your other income stays the same each year.
  • Investments: total return = price growth + dividends + interest. Dividends and interest are taxed yearly and reinvested; tax payments are assumed to come from new shares with little gain. Gains are taxed only when sold, all as long-term. Capital losses give no tax benefit here.
  • Loans: monthly amortization with the contractual note rate. APR is shown for reference only; fees on an existing loan are already paid. A prepayment keeps the payment and shortens the loan unless you choose recast. Variable rates change once; HELOC re-draws are not modeled.
  • Not modeled: state and local tax, Married Filing Separately and Head of Household, income-driven repayment and forgiveness for student loans, passive-loss rules and depreciation for rentals, §163(j) and self-employment tax for business loans, investment interest carryforward, refinancing, and the value of having cash on hand.
  • Labels: Verified rule = modeled from 2026 IRS rules; Simplified = a verified rule with parts left out; Your assumption = your input, not a legal conclusion.

Where the difference comes from

Invest minus Pay Off at the horizon, after tax if sold. The parts add up to the total. The two tax parts are split in a fixed order (deductions first, then investment income), so how they divide depends on that order; their sum does not.
