Assumptions
  • Fixed-rate loan paid monthly; each month's interest is the balance × APR ÷ 12. Extra money goes straight to principal and the monthly payment stays the same, so the loan ends sooner. No prepayment penalty.
  • Money market interest is taxed at the marginal rate you enter. Your actual after-tax yield depends on federal and state tax, the kind of fund (Treasury funds are often free of state tax), and your situation.
  • The loan's rate is not tax-adjusted, since car loan interest is usually not deductible.
  • All rates are annual rates compounded monthly, so they can be compared directly.
  • Keep an emergency fund: money paid into a loan can't be taken back out.

What does break-even mean here?
  • Car loan APR: the rate in your loan contract. Paying extra does not change it; it changes how much interest you pay and when the loan ends.
  • After-tax money market yield: what the same money would earn in the money market, after tax.
  • Return on paying extra: the annualized internal rate of return (IRR) of the extra payments compared with the regular schedule. That is the money you pay in early, and the payments you no longer owe once the loan ends sooner.
  • Break-even is the extra amount at which that return equals the after-tax money market yield. The calculator checks amounts from $0 up to paying the loan off entirely.
