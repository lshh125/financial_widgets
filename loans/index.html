Pay Off the Car Loan Early, or Keep the Cash in a Money Market?

Compares paying extra on a car loan now with leaving that cash in a money market fund. Both choices spend the same each month; once the loan is paid off early, its payment goes into the money market. Money market interest is taxed as ordinary income plus NIIT (2026 federal rules).

Taxes

Assumptions
  • Federal tax only, 2026 brackets with the standard deduction. Your tax rate is the bracket for your income; money market interest is taxed at that rate, plus 3.8% NIIT if your income is over $200k (single) or $250k (joint).
  • The APR is used as the loan's interest rate. Extra payments keep the monthly payment the same, so the loan ends sooner. A monthly extra is paid on top of the scheduled payment until the loan is gone; in the other choice the same amount goes into the money market each month. No prepayment penalty.
  • Money market interest compounds monthly and its tax is paid as it is earned. The rate stays the same; real money market yields move with short-term interest rates.
  • Car loan interest deduction (2025–2028 only): up to $10,000 a year for a new, U.S.-assembled, personal-use vehicle bought with a loan taken out after 2024. It shrinks by $200 per $1,000 of income over $100k (single) or $200k (joint). It does not reduce NIIT.
  • The comparison runs to the loan's original end date. State tax is not included (Treasury money market funds are often exempt from state tax, which would favor keeping the cash a little).
